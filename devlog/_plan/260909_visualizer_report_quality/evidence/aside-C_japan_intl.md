# Professional Report Architecture and Style: Japan and International Benchmarks

**Document Code:** `RESEARCH-260909-STYLE-C`  
**Date of Investigation:** September 9, 2026  
**Scope:** Structural, typographic, and rhetorical conventions across Japanese securities brokerages, Japanese government ministries, Japanese think tanks, global investment banks, legislative oversight bodies, multilateral institutions, and global strategy consultancies.

---

## 1. Findings: Structured Institutional Rules

Professional reports across major global jurisdictions and institutional archetypes follow rigorous, enforceable drafting standards designed to maximize reading speed, analytical accountability, legal defensibility, and accessibility. Below are the core operational rules established by primary authorities and verified from official guidebooks and report artifacts.

### 1.1 Japanese Financial Research (SAAJ, Nomura, Daiwa, SMBC Nikko)

1. **Mandatory Separation of Fact and Opinion:** Under the Securities Analysts Association of Japan (SAAJ) code of professional conduct, reports must strictly segregate objective empirical data from analyst forecasts and subjective evaluations.
   * *Rule:* Facts must be verifiable from public records; opinions, assumptions, and models must be labeled explicitly as analyst projections.
   * *Source:* SAAJ, *アナリスト・レポート作成の際の留意事項* (Guidelines for Preparing Analyst Reports), Section 1(2):
     > 「事実と意見を明確に区別すること （基準 3.（1）イ（ロ））」
   * *URL:* `https://www.saa.or.jp/learning/ethics/report/a/index.html` (Opened: 2026-09-09)

2. **Prohibition of Assertive or Guaranteering Language (断定的判断の提供等の禁止):** Japanese securities regulations and SAAJ guidelines strictly ban definitive statements regarding future stock prices, earnings forecasts, dividend policies, or capital restructuring.
   * *Rule:* Forward-looking outcomes cannot be phrased as certainties. Language guaranteeing return on investment is prohibited under JSDA self-regulatory rules and the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act.
   * *Source:* SAAJ, *アナリスト・レポート作成の際の留意事項*, Section 2(2)ロ:
     > 「個別企業の株価及び将来の業績、増減資、配当その他株価の騰落に相当な影響を及ぼす事項については、断定的な表示は行わない。」
   * *URL:* `https://www.saa.or.jp/learning/ethics/report/b/index.html` (Opened: 2026-09-09)

3. **Rejection of Rhetorical Fluff and Sensationalism (美辞麗句・扇情的見出しの排除):** SAAJ explicitly instructs analysts to reject rhetoric, flowery language, and sensational headlines intended to lure readers.
   * *Rule:* Content must be built through cumulative data and logic. Headings must convey calm, objective analysis rather than emotional or speculative triggers.
   * *Source:* SAAJ, *アナリスト・レポート作成の際の留意事項*, Section 3(1) and Section 3(6)c:
     > 「美辞麗句は避けることが望ましい。レポートの記述は、データの提示と論理を積み重ねて行うべきであり、修辞的な技巧に頼るのは望ましくない」
     > 「タイトル又は見出しの文章において、投資家の目を引く意図からセンセーショナルな表現をすることは、避けることが望ましい。」
   * *URL:* `https://www.saa.or.jp/learning/ethics/report/c/index.html` (Opened: 2026-09-09)

4. **Explicit Target Price Horizons and Rating Definitions:** Every sell-side brokerage report providing investment recommendations must publish the benchmark, the rating definitions, the price target valuation methodology, and the expected horizon (typically 12 months).
   * *Rule:* SMBC Nikko, Nomura, and Daiwa define ratings relative to market or sector coverage universe benchmarks (e.g., Outperform/Neutral/Underperform or Buy/Hold/Reduce), and must display a historical rating and target price track record.
   * *Sources:* 
     - SMBC Nikko Securities Inc., *Quarterly Economic Outlook for FY2025-2026*, p. 7-8 (`https://www.smbcnikko.co.jp/en/news/2026/pdf/260219_01e.pdf`, Opened: 2026-09-09).
     - Nomura International plc, *First Insights / Turkey: TCMB hopes to ride this wave out*, p. 3-4 (`https://www.nomura.com/events/emerging-markets-credit-conference/resources/upload/Turkey-TCMB-hopes-to-ride-this-wave-out.pdf`, Opened: 2026-09-09).

### 1.2 Japanese Government White Papers (経済産業省 METI, 総務省 MIC)

1. **Four-Tier Structural Hierarchy (部・章・節・項/目):** Official ministerial white papers follow a rigid legislative structural hierarchy: Part (部, Roman numerals), Chapter (章, Arabic), Section (節, Arabic), and Subsection/Item (項/目), frequently supplemented by thematic special features (特集) and case studies (コラム).
   * *Source:* Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI), *通商白書2024 目次* (White Paper on International Economy and Trade 2024 Table of Contents), p. 1-2 (`https://www.meti.go.jp/report/tsuhaku2024/pdf/mokuji.pdf`, Opened: 2026-09-09).
   * *Source:* Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC), *令和6年版 情報通信白書* (2024 White Paper on Information and Communications in Japan), p. 6-7 (`https://www.soumu.go.jp/johotsusintokei/whitepaper/ja/r06/pdf/00zentai.pdf`, Opened: 2026-09-09).

2. **Standardized Explanatory Conventions (凡例 Hanrei):** Japanese white papers open with a mandatory *Hanrei* section codifying abbreviations, statistical conventions, calendar definitions, and rounding disclosures before any analytical chapter begins.
   * *Rule on Calendar vs. Fiscal Years:* Unadorned years denote calendar years (Jan–Dec); fiscal years (April–March) must be explicitly designated as "年度".
   * *Rule on Rounding Discrepancies:* Figures are rounded off individually; totals in tables do not necessarily equal the sum of rounded constituents.
   * *Rule on Missing Data:* A single hyphen ("-") indicates unknown, non-calculable, or non-applicable data; "0" signifies a non-zero quantity below the smallest reported unit.
   * *Rule on As-of Information Cutoff Date:* The exact date of baseline data capture must be stated explicitly.
   * *Source:* METI, *通商白書2024 凡例* (Explanatory Notes), p. iv-v:
     > 「数値は、原則として四捨五入されており、合計とは一致しないことがある。」
     > 「図表中の記号のうち、数字が後に続かず単独で使われている「－」は特記しない限り不明、若しくは計上不能または該当事項なしを意味する。また、「0」は単位未満の数値を示す。」
     > 「本書は、特に断りがない限り、令和 6 年 3 月末時点で把握可能な情報を基に記載しています。」
   * *URL:* `https://www.meti.go.jp/report/tsuhaku2024/pdf/hanrei.pdf` (Opened: 2026-09-09)

3. **Hierarchical Compound Exhibit Numbering (複合図表番号):** Figures and tables are unified under the label `図表` and indexed to their exact structural parent (e.g., `図表Ⅰ-1-1-1` = Part I, Chapter 1, Section 1, Exhibit 1).
   * *Source:* MIC, *令和6年版 情報通信白書*, p. 15 (`https://www.soumu.go.jp/johotsusintokei/whitepaper/ja/r06/pdf/00zentai.pdf`, Opened: 2026-09-09).

### 1.3 Japanese Consultancies and Think Tanks (NRI, MRI)

1. **Policy-Oriented Synthesis and Propositional Headings (提言型見出し):** Japanese think tank reports (e.g., Mitsubishi Research Institute / MRI, Nomura Research Institute / NRI) structure their analytical flow around explicit policy recommendations (*提言*), formulating section headers as active policy proposals rather than passive topic descriptions.
   * *Rule:* Headings state the desired systemic intervention (e.g., "提言① CNと整合したCE政府目標を設定すべき" - *Recommendation 1: Set circular economy government targets aligned with carbon neutrality*).
   * *Source:* Mitsubishi Research Institute (MRI), *第7次エネルギー基本計画で求められる「CN×CE」の政策融合* (Policy Fusion of CN and CE Required for the 7th Strategic Energy Plan), p. ii, 12 (`https://www.mri.co.jp/knowledge/insight/policy/i5inlu000000arwl-att/20240605pec.pdf`, Opened: 2026-09-09).

2. **Multi-Author Affiliation Front Matter:** Japanese think tank publications list the full corporate division, group affiliation, and individual researcher titles on the front page or immediately under the title block to establish analytical accountability.
   * *Source:* Nomura Research Institute (NRI), *Exploration of Fulfillment Beyond the Digital Society*, p. 1 (`https://www.nri.com/content/900013080.pdf`, Opened: 2026-09-09).
   * *Source:* Daiwa Institute of Research (DIR), *Pathways for Financial and Capital Markets to Foster a “Virtuous Cycle of Asset Formation and Growth”*, p. 1 (`https://www.dir.co.jp/english/research/report/others/20260616_025831.pdf`, Opened: 2026-09-09).

### 1.4 Global Investment Banks (Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, J.P. Morgan)

1. **Cover-Page Executive Dashboard Architecture:** In sell-side investment banking research (J.P. Morgan, Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley), Page 1 serves as an executive decision portal that encapsulates 80% of actionable conclusions before page turn.
   * *Rule:* Page 1 must display: Ticker, Rating, Current Price, Target Price, Expected Return, Price Performance 12-month graph, Core Valuation Multiples (P/E, EV/EBITDA, FCF yield), Investment Thesis Summary (1-2 paragraphs), and 3-4 bulleted catalysts/earnings drivers.
   * *Source:* J.P. Morgan Europe Equity Research, *Ryanair: Trimming estimates and PT on cautious fare guidance, but still see c.20% upside potential*, p. 1 (`https://investor.ryanair.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/J.P.-Morgan-2016-05-26.pdf`, Opened: 2026-09-09).

2. **The "Call" Framework for Collaborative Thematic Reports:** Multi-sector global deep-dives (such as Morgan Stanley Blue Papers) address three explicit structural questions in the executive summary:
   1. *Why now?* (The catalyst or disruption forcing an immediate reassessment)
   2. *What is the framework?* (The proprietary economic or total addressable market model)
   3. *Who is exposed?* (The specific winners, losers, and stock portfolio across the value chain)
   * *Source:* Morgan Stanley Research, *Blue Paper: The New Oil: Investment Implications of the Global Battery Economy*, p. 6:
     > "Like any good piece of research, we want to answer three questions: (1) Why now?, (2) What's the framework?, and (3) Who's exposed? To that end, we built a proprietary battery total addressable market (TAM) model and a global battery stock portfolio."
   * *URL:* `https://advisor.morganstanley.com/eliah-najjar/documents/field/l/la/lanigan---najjar-group/Morgan%20Stanley%20Blue%20Paper%20-%20The%20Electric%20Battery%20Economy%2011-2021.pdf` (Opened: 2026-09-09)

3. **Mandatory Conflict Disclosures and Analyst Certifications:** Global investment bank research must carry strict disclaimers governed by SEC Rule 17a-4, FINRA Rule 2241, and European MAR, placed both on the front cover (summary warning) and back matter (full legal disclosures).
   * *Rule:* Front cover must declare: "Investors should be aware that the firm may have a conflict of interest that could affect the objectivity of this report. Investors should consider this report as only a single factor in making their investment decision."
   * *Rule:* Analysts marked with "[AC]" must certify that views accurately reflect personal beliefs and that compensation was not directly linked to specific investment banking deals.
   * *Source:* Goldman Sachs Global Investment Research, *Top of Mind: AI: In a Bubble?*, Issue 143, p. 1, 26-28 (`https://www.goldmansachs.com/pdfs/insights/goldman-sachs-research/ai-in-a-bubble/report.pdf`, Opened: 2026-09-09).
   * *Source:* J.P. Morgan Europe Equity Research, p. 1, 8-11.

### 1.5 Legislative Oversight and Public Administration (US GAO, UK GOV.UK / GCS)

1. **The Three-Part GAO Highlights Page:** Every report by the United States Government Accountability Office (GAO) begins on page i with a standardized, single-page summary organized into three mandatory sections:
   * *Why GAO Did This Study:* States the statutory mandate, congressional committee request, background context, and investigative methodology.
   * *What GAO Found:* Concrete empirical findings, identified deficiencies, and quantified fiscal impacts.
   * *What GAO Recommends:* Specific, numbered action items addressed to agency heads or legislative matters for Congress.
   * *Source:* US Government Accountability Office, *GAO-14-225: Credit Cards: Marketing to College Students Appears to Have Declined*, p. 2 (Highlights):
     > "Trends associated with college affinity card agreements include fewer agreements and cardholders and declining payments, according to data GAO analyzed from the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System..."
   * *URL:* `https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-14-225.pdf` (Opened: 2026-09-09)

2. **GOV.UK Plain English Mandate:** The UK Government Digital Service (GDS) and Government Communication Service (GCS) mandate strict readability thresholds across all government communications:
   * *Rule:* 80% of readers (including subject-matter specialists) prefer plain English because it enables faster comprehension.
   * *Rule:* Paragraphs must contain no more than 5 sentences.
   * *Rule:* Sentences exceeding 25 words must be split or edited down.
   * *Rule:* Active voice is mandatory; passive voice is restricted to rare instances where the action's recipient is the central subject.
   * *Source:* UK Government Digital Service, *GOV.UK Content and Publishing Guidance: Clear Language*:
     > "Paragraphs should have no more than 5 sentences each. Try to split up sentences that are over 25 words long."
   * *URL:* `https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language` (Opened: 2026-09-09)

3. **Digital Accessibility and Heading Semantics:** UK government publishing rules prohibit using visual bolding for headings; semantic heading levels (`H2`, `H3`, `H4`) must be strictly maintained without skipping levels to ensure screen-reader accessibility under WCAG 2.2.
   * *Rule:* Footnotes must be avoided in digital documents; contextual explanations must be embedded inline.
   * *Source:* UK GDS & CDDO, *Publishing Accessible Documents*:
     > "Do not use bold to mark up subheadings. Use styles to create a hierarchy of headings: ‘heading 1’, ‘heading 2’ and so on... Avoid footnotes where possible. Provide explanations inline instead."
   * *URL:* `https://www.gov.uk/guidance/publishing-accessible-documents` (Opened: 2026-09-09)

### 1.6 Multilateral Institutions (World Bank, IMF, OECD)

1. **Unnumbered Headings Policy:** Multilateral policy organizations reject numerical heading schemes (1.1, 1.1.1) in thematic publications, relying instead on visual typography and headline-style capitalization to establish hierarchy.
   * *World Bank Rule:* "In general, headings should not be numbered. However, numbering may make sense for some publications, such as handbooks." (World Bank Style Guide 2020, Section 11.3, p. 50).
   * *IMF Rule:* "Use headline-style capitalization for each level of heading. Do not number headings." (IMF Editorial Style Guide 2024, p. 5).
   * *OECD Rule:* "Words in a heading: 3-5... Avoid multiple headings, which can disorient readers: use subheadings sparingly to signpost key elements." (OECD Style Guide, 3rd ed., p. 22, 33).

2. **What-Where-When Exhibit Caption Formula:** Tables and figures in multilateral publications must follow an informative descriptive title formula answering three questions: What is measured, Where (geography), and When (time frame).
   * *World Bank Rule:* "Titles should be descriptive, covering 'What,' 'Where,' and 'When.' They should not include background information, repeat column heads, or describe results illustrated by the table." (World Bank Style Guide 2020, Section 14.6, p. 64).
   * *IMF Rule:* "Include descriptive but concise titles, following the what-where-when format." (IMF Editorial Style Guide 2024, Section 5, p. 13).

3. **Mandatory Exhibit Source Line Standards:** Every exhibit must carry a standardized source line directly beneath the graphic.
   * *Rule:* Use singular "Source:" for one entity; plural "Sources:" when combining multiple authorities. In-house data manipulation must be designated explicitly as "World Bank staff calculations" or "IMF staff estimates."
   * *Source:* IMF Editorial Style Guide 2024, Section 5, p. 13:
     > "All boxes, figures, and tables require a source line. Use the plural “Sources:” if more than one source is cited."
   * *Source:* World Bank Style Guide 2020, Section 14.18, p. 66:
     > "Source: De la Fuente 2015. Sources: De la Fuente 2015; World Bank 2018. Sources: World Bank staff calculations based on data from..."

### 1.7 Global Strategy Consultancies (Deloitte, PwC, EY)

1. **Narrative-Driven Declarative Headings:** Big Four thought leadership replaces passive topic headers with active declarative sentences or challenging strategic questions.
   * *Deloitte Example:* "When people thrive, business thrives: The case for human sustainability" / "The transparency paradox: Could less be more when it comes to trust?" (Deloitte Insights, *2024 Global Human Capital Trends*, p. 1).
   * *PwC Example:* "Leading through uncertainty in the age of AI" / "Confidence down, threats up" / "The road to reinvention" (PwC, *29th Global CEO Survey*, p. 2).
   * *EY Example:* "Are you reframing your future or is the future reframing you?" (EY, *Megatrends 2020 and Beyond*, p. 1).

2. **Survey Sample Transparency and Weighting Disclosures:** Large-scale management surveys must publish a standalone methodology section detailing the sample size, geographic distribution, respondent seniority, and GDP-weighting formulas.
   * *Source:* PwC, *29th Global CEO Survey*, p. 23:
     > "PwC surveyed 4,454 CEOs in 95 countries and territories... The global and regional figures in this report are weighted proportionally to country nominal GDP, so CEOs’ views are broadly representative across all major regions."

### Detailed Zone-by-Zone Anatomy

#### 1. Cover / Title Page
* **Model A (Sell-Side IB Note):** There is no separate cover sheet. Page 1 combines brand banner, date, sector, company name, ticker, rating, target price, analyst contact card, price chart, and executive thesis.
* **Model B (Thematic Blue Paper):** Clean full-bleed cover. Minimalist typography, overarching report title, subtitle, date/time stamp, primary sector tag, and a mandatory bottom disclaimer box regarding conflicts of interest. Page 2 is a dedicated "Contributors" directory with analyst contacts across regional desks.
* **Model C (Japan White Paper):** Formal bureaucratic cover. Full official title (e.g., `令和6年版 情報通信白書`), subtitle, ministry attribution (`総務省 編` or `経済産業省`), and era-based publication date.
* **Model D (US GAO Report):** Highly structured standard template. Top agency banner ("United States Government Accountability Office"), addressee line ("Report to Congressional Committees"), publication month/year, report title in all caps, and unique document identifier (e.g., `GAO-14-225`).
* **Model E (Multilateral Flagship):** Book cover format. Flagship brand title (e.g., *World Development Report* or *World Economic Outlook*), subtitle, organization logo, followed by formal interior title page with publisher imprint, cataloging-in-publication data, and ISBN/DOI.
* **Model F (Strategy Thought Leadership):** Consumer magazine style. Bold graphic hero artwork, provocative question or declarative title, firm branding (e.g., *Deloitte Insights*, *PwC Strategy&*), and publication year.

#### 2. Contents and Front Matter
* **Model A:** Omitted due to short length (1-10 pages). Internal sections use bold paragraph headers or subheadings.
* **Model B:** High-level table of contents on page 3-5, divided into thematic chapters, followed by a directory of featured corporate profiles or stock portfolio listings.
* **Model C:** Multi-tiered front matter consisting of: (1) Minister's Foreword (`公表にあたって`), (2) Executive Summary (`白書の概要`), (3) Master Table of Contents (`総目次`), (4) Detailed Table of Contents (`目次`), and (5) Explanatory Notes (`凡例`).
* **Model D:** Table of Contents on page i-ii listing: Transmittal Letter, Chapter Titles, Appendices, List of Tables, List of Figures, and Abbreviations list. Front matter numbered in Roman numerals (`Page i`, `Page ii`).
* **Model E:** Formal publishing front matter: Foreword by President/Managing Director, Acknowledgments, Table of Contents (listing chapters, level A headings only, boxes, figures, tables), Abbreviations / Acronyms list, and Executive Summary.
* **Model F:** Stylized editorial contents page with large numeric callouts (`01`, `02`, `03`) and evocative chapter titles, often accompanied by an introductory letter from senior leadership.

#### 3. Executive Summary
* **Model A:** Embedded on Page 1 as a lead synopsis paragraph (bold or boxed) accompanied by 3-5 bulleted core investment arguments.
* **Model B:** 2-4 page opening section titled "The Call" or "Executive Summary" answering *Why Now?*, *What's the Framework?*, and *Who's Exposed?*, featuring key infographics.
* **Model C:** Visual standalone summary booklet or opening 2-6 page briefing section (`白書の概要`) summarizing key findings by Part (部) and Chapter (章) with miniature callout charts.
* **Model D:** Standalone single-page "Highlights" page on page 2 (before the transmittal letter) split into: "Why GAO Did This Study", "What GAO Found", and "What GAO Recommends".
* **Model E:** 5-15 page "Overview" or "Executive Summary" synthesizing cross-country macro trends, structural imbalances, and priority policy actions.
* **Model F:** 1-2 page narrative framing ("Foreword" or "Executive Summary") summarizing empirical survey results with bolded bullet points and large stat callouts.

#### 4. Body Architecture
* **Model A:** Tightly packed 2-column or 3-column layout. Primary analysis, earnings revisions, valuation discussion (DCF, multiples), and key downside/upside risks.
* **Model B:** Thematic chapters spanning macroeconomic drivers, supply-chain analysis, sector impacts, and detailed company deep-dives. Heavily populated with numbered exhibits.
* **Model C:** Strict bureaucratic hierarchy: `第Ⅰ部` -> `第1章` -> `第1節` -> `1.` -> `(1)` -> `①`. Body text is neutral and descriptive, systematically supported by compound-numbered exhibits (`図表1-1-1`).
* **Model D:** Begins with formal transmittal letter addressed to Congressional Committee Chairs and Ranking Members, transitioning into body sections headed by full declarative sentence statements.
* **Model E:** Chapters structured around core policy themes. Headings are unnumbered headline-style titles. Analytical narrative is supported by themed text boxes, data tables, and figures.
* **Model F:** Magazine-style layout with pull quotes, infographic diagrams, case study sidebars, and prominent subheads designed for non-linear skimming.

#### 5. Appendix / Back Matter
* **Model A:** Valuation methodology descriptions, financial model summary statements, and historical recommendation change tables.
* **Model B:** Extensive stock tables, scenario sensitivity models, proprietary survey methodologies, and exhaustive company directories.
* **Model C:** Endnotes/Annotations (`付注`), Index of Figures and Tables (`図表索引`), Special Survey questionnaires, and legislative reference texts.
* **Model D:** "Appendix I: Objectives, Scope, and Methodology" (auditing protocol), "Appendix II: Agency Comments" (reproduced letters from evaluated departments), and "Appendix III: GAO Contacts and Staff Acknowledgments".
* **Model E:** Technical Annexes (statistical models, econometric specifications), Country Classification tables, Statistical Appendix (macro indicators), and Bibliography / Reference List.
* **Model F:** Detailed survey methodology (sample demographics, weighting formulas), acknowledgments, author biographies, and research center directories.

#### 6. Legal Disclaimers and Compliance
* **Model A:** 2-4 pages of mandatory end matter: Analyst Certification, Rating Distribution Table, Conflict Disclosures (investment banking client fees, market-making status), and regional legal notices across 15+ jurisdictions.
* **Model B:** Extensive 10-20 page disclosure appendix at the end of the book detailing individual coverage status for all covered tickers mentioned in the text.
* **Model C:** Government works notice. Public copyright waiver (`総務省 編` / `経済産業省`), citation guidelines, and publisher contact information.
* **Model D:** Public domain notice: "This is a work of the U.S. government and is not subject to copyright protection in the United States...".
* **Model E:** Formal copyright and licensing notice (Creative Commons CC-BY 3.0 IGO), disclaimer on territorial borders and sovereignty ("The findings, interpretations, and conclusions expressed in this work do not necessarily reflect the views of..."), and cataloging disclosures.
* **Model F:** Intellectual property notice, corporate structure disclaimer ("PwC refers to the PwC network and/or one or more of its member firms..."), and liability waiver ("This publication contains general information only and is not intended to provide professional advice...").

## 3. Writing Rules across Professional Traditions

### 3.1 Title and Heading Form

| Dimension | Wall Street / IB Research | Japanese White Papers | US GAO Reports | Multilateral Flagships | Strategy Consultancies |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **H1 (Document Title)** | Imperative claim or topic thesis | Formal administrative title | All-caps topic plus subtitle | Evocative thematic title | Provocative question or bold statement |
| **Section Headings** | Declarative sentence or active phrase | Numeric-hierarchical (部/章/節) | Complete declarative sentence | Concise topical heading (3-5 words) | Declarative assertion or question |
| **Numbering Scheme** | Unnumbered | Numbered (`第1章 第1節`) | Unnumbered in body; lettered apps | Strictly unnumbered | Numbered or unnumbered |
| **Capitalization** | Headline Style (Title Case) | Standard Japanese script | Sentence case or headline style | Headline Style (Chicago Manual) | Sentence case or headline style |

* **Length Limits:** OECD establishes a strict quantitative guideline:
  * *Titles:* 8–10 words.
  * *Headings:* 3–5 words.
  * *Abstracts:* 20 words or less.
* **Headlines as Arguments:** In US GAO and investment banking research, section headings are active arguments, not generic subject tags.
  * *Bad:* "Credit Card Marketing".
  * *Good (GAO):* "Marketing of Credit Cards Specifically to Students Has Declined, According to Issuers and Survey Data" (GAO-14-225, p. 19).
  * *Good (JPM):* "Trimming estimates and PT on cautious fare guidance, but still see c.20% upside potential" (JPMorgan, p. 1).

### 3.2 Paragraph Conventions

1. **The Single-Idea Rule:** Every paragraph must focus on a single core thesis. The opening sentence must articulate the main point; subsequent sentences provide supporting evidence, qualifications, or quantification.
   * *OECD Rule:* "Keep paragraphs down to single ideas. Each paragraph should start with the main idea of the paragraph. Subsequent sentences in the paragraph should contain details the reader needs. Discuss new topics in new paragraphs." (OECD Style Guide, p. 22).
2. **Paragraph Sentence Budgets:**
   * *UK GOV.UK:* Maximum 5 sentences per paragraph. Chunks of text must remain short to accommodate mobile and accessible screen-reader scanning.
   * *Wall Street Research:* Lead summary paragraphs are capped at 3–4 sentences, supplemented by bullet points for discrete catalysts.
3. **Inverted Pyramid Ordering:** In all analytical writing, put the bottom line first. Avoid background build-up that delays findings. The conclusion opens the paragraph; the proof follows.

### 3.3 Sentence Conventions

1. **Sentence Length Ceilings:**
   * *UK GOV.UK:* Strict limit of 25 words per sentence. Sentences exceeding 25 words must be split or rewritten.
   * *OECD:* Sentences over 20 words are considered difficult to follow.
2. **Active Voice Primacy:** Put the actor before the verb.
   * *Passive:* "An electronic travel authorisation is needed by travelers."
   * *Active:* "You need an electronic travel authorisation." (GOV.UK Clear Language).
   * *Passive:* "A miss of FY16 net income was delivered by RYA."
   * *Active:* "RYA delivered a modest FY16 miss on Monday relative to our expectations." (J.P. Morgan, p. 1).
3. **Emphatic Word Positioning:** Place the most critical, impactful word at the end of the sentence. Words buried in the middle lose emphasis.
   * *OECD Rule:* "Place emphatic words at the end of the sentence. In the middle, they will be swallowed up: *The problem must be solved immediately* [not *The problem must be immediately solved*]." (OECD Style Guide, p. 22).
4. **Jargon and Buzzword Elimination:**
   * Replace nominalizations (words ending in *-tion*, *-ment*) with direct verbs (use *decide* instead of *make a decision*; *analyse* instead of *perform an analysis of*).
   * Avoid bureaucratic filler (*it should be noted that*, *it is important to add that*, *at the outset*).

### 3.4 Number, Formula, and Evidence Conventions

1. **Words vs. Numerals Thresholds:**
   * *UK GOV.UK / GCS:* Spell out "one"; use numerals for all numbers from 2 to 999,999 (`2`, `9`, `25`, `1,000`). If a number starts a sentence, write it out in full (e.g., `Thirty-four`), except when it starts a heading.
   * *World Bank / Chicago Manual:* Spell out whole numbers from zero through one hundred; use numerals for 101 and above.
   * *ONS / UK Civil Service:* Write numbers zero to nine as words, 10 and above as numerals.
   * *METI / Japanese Practice:* Numerals are used throughout technical text and tables; full-width Arabic numerals are standardized in horizontal text.
2. **Percentages and Mathematical Operators:**
   * *UK GOV.UK / Big Four:* Use the `%` symbol directly with numerals (`50%`, `0.7%`).
   * *World Bank / IMF Style:* Use the word "percent" with numbers in running text (`5 percent`); use the symbol `%` only in tables, figures, or parenthetical references. Distinguish strictly between *percent* and *percentage points* (`a 2 percentage point increase`).
   * *Multiplication / Dimensions:* Use the mathematical symbol `×` (Unicode U+00D7), never the letter `x` (e.g., `10 × 20 mm`, not `10 x 20 mm`).
3. **Number Ranges and Spans:**
   * *UK Standards:* Always use "to" rather than a hyphen or en dash in running text (`500 to 900`, `aged 19 to 30 years`, `Monday to Friday`). En dashes are permitted only in tabular data.
   * *Japanese Standards:* Use the wave dash (`〜`) or hyphen (`-`) for ranges in tables, but write out `から` or `〜` in body text.
4. **Rounding and Missing Data Syntax:**
   * Disclose rounding policies explicitly in notes (`Figures are rounded; sums may not equal totals`).
   * Follow METI/MIC standards: Use a solitary hyphen (`-`) for non-existent or unmeasurable data; use `0` exclusively for quantities smaller than the reported unit.
5. **Exhibit Titles and Source Line Attribution:**
   * Titles must answer *What, Where, When* (e.g., *Table 1: Real GDP Growth in Selected East Asian Economies, 2020–2025*).
   * Source lines are mandatory directly beneath every exhibit:
     * *Single Source:* `Source: Bank of Japan.` / `（出典）内閣府等資料を基に作成`
     * *Multiple Sources:* `Sources: Haver Analytics; World Bank; and IMF staff calculations.`
